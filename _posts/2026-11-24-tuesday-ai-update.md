Anthropic sold 980 million shares at $83 on 18 November and raised $81.3bn, the largest IPO by proceeds on record ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/anthropic-reportedly-prices-ipo-at-83-a-share-above-projected-range/)). By Friday the Nasdaq had closed its worst week since March 2020, down 10%, with Nvidia down nearly 40% for the month ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/20/sp-500-correction-market-sell-off.html)).

Two frontier labs cut prices in the same seven days. The companies that build servers asked up to 45% more. Cheaper tokens, dearer metal.

## The week in AI

### Western: a debut, a drawdown, a discount

- **OpenAI shipped GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol on 17 November.** Astra costs $8 and $40 per million input and output tokens, 20% below GPT-6 Astra with a 60% cached-input discount; Sol costs $2 and $10. Artificial Analysis puts Astra at 1,318 on its human-voted index, ahead of Claude Fable 5.2 at 1,271 ([The Rundown](https://www.therundown.ai/news/openai-gpt-6-2-astra-sol-reviews-anthropic-ipo)). Both meet the Critical cybersecurity threshold in OpenAI's Preparedness Framework, and OpenAI calls Astra the first released model at its High self-improvement threshold. Its own page concedes that both omit blockers more often than their predecessors, the failure that matters when work runs unattended.
- **Google answered a day later with Gemini 4 Neon at $3 and $15 per million tokens**, with a 1M-token context. It scores 61 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at xhigh thinking, third overall, and tops the AA Coding Agent Index at 58.6 with 65.2% on Terminal-Bench 4.1. Google says it costs 32% less per task than Gemini 4 Carbon and scores 0.0% on Gray Swan's indirect prompt injection benchmark ([MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/11/18/google-gemini-4-neon-release/)).
- **Anthropic's shares ran from $83 to above $115 on debut and closed the week at $88.91.** The S&P 500 and Dow finished in correction. Nvidia fell 14% on Friday after Reuters reported the SEC is examining its accounting; its board reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive on 23 November, saying his return poses no national security risk.

### China: a loop, and a licence ledger

- **Z.ai released GLM-5.4 on 23 November**, a 750B-scale model that runs 28 shared layers of the GLM-5 base twice per forward pass. It was adapted from the pretrained base rather than trained from scratch, and Z.ai claims parity with 2T to 3T models at modest compute overhead. Hosted only, no weights published ([MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/11/23/zai-glm-5-4-loop-transformer/)). In Z.ai's own looped ablation the gain ran from +11.3 points on AIME25 to +3.3 on IFBench, so the second pass buys reasoning, not instruction-following.
- **The House Foreign Affairs Committee released export-licence data on 19 November.** Companies requested 3.4 million AI chips for China between December and May; 879,868 were approved as of 11 May, including sales to firms the Defense Department designates as Chinese Military Companies. Alibaba received 75,000 of roughly 950,000 it asked for, Tencent 150,000, Baidu 43,000, BYD 4,000 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/19/nvidia-amd-china-chip-sales-congress.html)). Beijing never cleared large-scale imports, analysts told CNBC, so the approvals overstate what shipped.

### Europe: a venue, not yet a date

- **Arthur Mensch said Mistral is preparing to go public and that "the natural place to list is Europe"**, speaking at Semafor's Silicon Valley & The World event on 17 November ([Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/11/17/2026/europe-the-natural-location-for-mistral-ipo-ceo-says)). He gave no date, and MLQ reported the listing will not happen this year.

### MENA: the state as a product surface

- **The UAE unveiled Mira, a government AI assistant, in Dubai on 23 November.** The first phase arrives in March 2027 and covers residency visas, Emirates ID renewals, passport services, healthcare and education, with every government service routed through it by the end of that year ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/uae/dubai/uae-launches-mira-ai-assistant-for-government-services-1.500757702)). Officials describe it as anticipating a need and completing supported transactions, rather than waiting for a query.

### Africa: a deadline, a raise, a draft

- **Kenya's High Court gave the executive and Parliament six months, to 13 May 2027, to finish a national AI policy and legislation.** Justice Edward Muriithi, in Kerugoya, declined to halt deployments but listed the systems the petition named: the Social Health Authority's automated household means-testing, facial recognition, predictive policing, automated exam marking and AI-assisted recruitment. The petitioners had asked for 90 days ([Nairobi Wire](https://nairobiwire.com/2026/11/high-court-orders-ai-legal-framework-six-months.html)).
- **Cassava Technologies is seeking up to $1bn**, most of it for Nvidia chips for its African AI data centres, with the rest for fibre and facilities. The company declined to comment and the round may not close ([Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/11/18/2026/cassava-technologies-seeks-1b-for-nvidia-chips)).
- **The East African Community has a draft regional AI strategy before ministers**, with no adoption date. Benin, running a 123-action strategy adopted in 2023 on about 4.68 billion CFA francs, is classifying ministry data to decide what stays on its servers; 550-plus services are already online and the criteria are unpublished ([Innovation Village](https://innovation-village.com/ai-africa-intelligence-november-12-18-2026-vol-30/)).
- **Huawei's Yongqiang Yuan told the WBBA Broadband Development Congress in Cape Town that Africa records more than 100,000 fibre cuts a year**, and that a dollar spent on resilience saves five in losses ([The Citizen](https://www.citizen.co.za/lifestyle/technology/africas-ai-dreams-at-risk-100000-annual-fibre-cuts-expose-broadband-weakness/)).

### South America: capacity with a jurisdiction attached

- **Google Cloud will double its infrastructure in Brazil by 2030** and switched on in-country processing for Gemini 3.5 Flash inside Gemini Enterprise, plus G4 virtual machines on Nvidia RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell GPUs in São Paulo. Its commissioned IDC and Provokers study found 62% of Brazilian organisations accelerating agent adoption, 17% with governance consolidated ([Intelligent CIO](https://www.intelligentcio.com/latam/2026/11/20/google-cloud-doubles-down-on-brazil-with-major-agentic-ai-expansion/)).

## Spotlight: what the float priced

One detail of the Anthropic pricing was unusual. The company raised its offer from 825 million shares to 980 million the day before pricing, then priced above range at $83 anyway. That produced $81.3bn, ahead of SpaceX's $75bn, a fully diluted valuation above $1.5 trillion, up from $965bn in May, and a $26bn private placement of non-voting shares alongside. Amazon holds nearly 15% and Google almost 14% ([New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/18/technology/anthropic-ipo-pricing.html)).

The tape reads differently from the pricing. The stock cleared $115 intraday, then finished the week at $88.91, near the offer price and well under the debut pop. When the largest listing on record cannot hold its first week, buyers treated it as the exit rather than the entry.

Three things landed together. OpenAI cut its frontier price the day before the roadshow closed. Dell, HPE, Lenovo and Supermicro asked up to 45% more for machines already under contract. The SEC opened an accounting examination at the company that supplies the chips. One variable: cost per token ([TopFive](https://topfive.ai/2026/11/23/)).

Nothing in that sequence changes what a builder can buy today. Gemini 4 Neon at $3 and $15, GPT-6.2 Sol at $2 and $10 and a hosted GLM-5.4 are all cheaper than what they replaced. Whether cheaper tokens expand demand faster than dearer hardware compresses margin is now the whole trade, and the week gave no answer.

## Why it matters for Africa

Three items this week set terms for African builders, and none of them is a model.

The Kenya ruling puts a date on compliance. By 13 May 2027 there must be a framework covering the systems already in production, from household means-testing to exam marking. Anyone selling AI into Kenyan public services has a schedule to plan against, and a list of uses a court has already examined.

Cassava's $1bn shows where the money goes. Most of it buys chips abroad, and a 45% post-contract repricing lands on the buyer mid-build. The model layer is the one input getting cheaper, which argues for renting inference while owning the product.

The layer underneath matters too. A continent recording more than 100,000 fibre cuts a year can host inference it cannot reliably reach. Benin's unpublished data classification and Google's in-country processing in Brazil are one story at different latitudes: whoever decides where data is processed decides who can be served.
