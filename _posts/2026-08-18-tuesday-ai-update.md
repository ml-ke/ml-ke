## The Month in AI

August 2026 will be remembered as the month the centre of gravity shifted. Chinese labs shipped frontier-adjacent models at prices that make Western flagships look like luxury goods, Europe began enforcing the AI Act's transparency rules, and Brazil placed one of the biggest AI infrastructure bets of the quarter. From Nairobi's draft AI policy to Sber's GigaChat update in Moscow, here is the global roundup across seven regions.

### Asia & China: The Price War Goes Nuclear

DeepSeek made it official this month: V4-Pro, previewed since April, is now generally available across app, web and API ([Quartz, Aug 13](https://qz.com/deepseek-v4-pro-official-launch-081326)). The pricing is the headline — Axios calculates DeepSeek charges about 28 cents for output that costs $25 on Anthropic's Opus 4.8, a ~99% discount ([Axios, Aug 1](https://www.axios.com/2026/08/01/deepseek-model-cheap-ai-price-war)). From August 16 the API moves to peak/off-peak pricing, with off-peak at half the peak rate ([DeepSeek API docs](https://api-docs.deepseek.com/updates/)).

Moonshot's Kimi K3, the largest open-source model release ever, continues to send ripples through Silicon Valley ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/07/26/china-moonshot-deepseek-zai-kimi-challenging-us-ai-cost/)), and Bloomberg reports Moonshot and Z.AI are now close to OpenAI and Anthropic on performance ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-08-20/moonshot-zai-close-to-openai-and-anthropic-model-performance)). Alibaba unveiled Qwen3.8-Max on August 3, sending its shares up ~6% in Hong Kong ([Yahoo Finance](https://ca.finance.yahoo.com/news/alibaba-stock-soars-unveiling-qwen-121120900.html)), and Reuters reports it may ask big users of the next open-source Qwen for a share of revenue ([Reuters, Aug 7](https://www.reuters.com/business/retail-consumer/alibaba-plans-charge-big-users-its-next-open-source-ai-model-sources-say-2026-08-07/)). Bloomberg notes Alibaba, Baidu and Kuaishou are now wrestling with the mounting cost of the race — the same pressure that dented Tencent's shares ([Bloomberg, Aug 14](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-14/alibaba-baidu-kuaishou-address-mounting-ai-costs-as-competition-intensifies)), while Kuaishou's Kling AI 3.0 video suite remains the generation benchmark to beat ([Kuaishou IR](https://ir.kuaishou.com/news-releases/news-release-details/kling-ai-launches-30-model-ushering-era-where-everyone-can-be)).

### Europe: Enforcement Day Arrives

The EU AI Act's transparency obligations took effect on August 2, with the AI Office and national authorities beginning enforcement — chatbots must now disclose they are AI, and deepfakes must be labelled ([European Commission](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_26_1714)). Mistral shipped Leanstral 1.5, an updated Lean 4 formal proof engineering model ([Mistral release notes](https://releasebot.io/updates/mistral)), and continues its sovereign-AI push with regional inference endpoints, open models and a coalition for European compute, plus plans for a 10 MW data centre in France ([Mistral news](https://mistral.ai/news/regional-inference-open-models-new-compute/)).

In applied AI, Synthesia — valued at $4 billion after its Series E — expanded to Seattle with Amazon veteran Brett Taylor leading its new West Coast engineering team ([Synthesia blog](https://www.synthesia.io/blog/category/synthesia)) and deepened its multilingual video partnership with DeepL ([DeepL blog](https://www.deepl.com/en/blog/synthesia-deepl-partner-video-innovation)). ElevenLabs kept shipping on the audio side, updating ElevenAgents and speech-to-text across its August changelog ([ElevenLabs changelog](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/changelog)).

### MENA: Infrastructure and Sovereign Platforms

G42's Core42 is scaling beyond the Gulf: it established a European headquarters in Dublin, is expanding its New York deployment ([G42](https://www.g42.ai/resources/news/core42-establishes-european-headquarters-dublin-support-growing-demand-ai-infrastructure)), and leased 20 MW in a converted Minneapolis office building — part of a 200 MW Nvidia Grace Blackwell GB300 cluster scheduled for end-2026, with build-out toward one gigawatt ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/core42-g42-minneapolis-data-center-office-conversion-uae)). In the Emirates, the Cyber Security Council, e& UAE and Open Innovation AI launched the UAE Sovereign AI Platform for secure national-scale infrastructure ([StartupScene](https://thestartupscene.me/MenaEcosystems/UAE-Launches-Sovereign-AI-Platform-for-Secure-Infrastructure)).

Saudi Arabia's momentum shows in the World Bank's World Development Report 2026, which highlights the Kingdom's AI investment and talent pull ([SPA, Aug 12](https://www.spa.gov.sa/en/N2652651)), and in the Cultural Development Fund's new grants of up to SR500,000 for AI-powered cultural projects, open August 10–29 September ([Saudi Gazette](https://saudigazette.com.sa/article/663650/saudi-arabia/saudi-arabia-launches-ai-incentives-offering-up-to-sr500000-for-cultural-projects)). In Egypt, Efham.ai plans 100+ Arabic AI training lessons by Q3 2026 across Egypt, Saudi Arabia and the wider Arab market ([Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2646163/business-economy)), while Israeli deeptech keeps drawing the biggest cheques — startups like Arrakis Security are building security layers for what autonomous AI agents actually do ([Calcalist](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/rq8lzbs4c)).

### Africa: Policy and Homegrown Builders

Kenya published its draft AI and Emerging Technologies Policy for public consultation (closing August 4), proposing a national emerging-technologies council, risk-based classification and regulatory sandboxes — while Côte d'Ivoire approved a $170 million data centre ([Africa AI News](https://www.africaainews.com/p/kenya-shares-draft-ai-policy-cote), [Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/07/28/kenya-ai-policy-2026-public-consultation/)). The nuance: Rest of World notes Nigeria, Kenya, Egypt and South Africa still lean heavily on Google, Microsoft, Nvidia and Meta for models and compute, even as policies flag that dependence as a security risk ([Rest of World](https://restofworld.org/2026/africa-ai-sovereignty-big-tech/)).

On the private side, Cape Town's Cue raised $5 million, co-led by Knife Capital and FAM Investments, to scale autonomous AI customer-service agents across South Africa and the UK ([allAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202607220726.html)), and ALX is scaling its enterprise offering to build AI-ready workforces across the continent ([Citi Newsroom](https://www.citinewsroom.com/2026/08/alx-scales-its-enterprise-offering-to-build-ai-ready-workforces-across-africa/)). Google's Africa Applied AI Lab — run with 4DX Ventures and partners — keeps applications open until August 31, offering early model access and mentorship ([Google Labs](https://labs.google/aifuturesfund/africaailab), [Disrupt Africa](https://disruptafrica.com/2026/07/28/applications-open-for-google-africa-applied-ai-lab/)). Johannesburg's Lelapa AI remains the flagship for African-language NLP with its Vulavula platform, holding the "built by Africans, for Africans" line ([Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/technology/african-tech-startups-take-aim-at-ai-colonialism/articleshow/102562664.cms)).

### South America: Brazil's Supercomputer Gambit

The biggest story south of the equator is Brazil's 2.3 billion reais (~$444 million) AI supercomputer push, announced August 20: one system built with Huawei, a second expected to go to Nvidia — a deliberate hedge against dependence on any single vendor, with the Nvidia machine slated for Rio Grande do Norte ([Reuters, Aug 20](https://www.reuters.com/world/americas/brazil-launches-ai-supercomputer-push-splits-projects-between-chinese-us-firms-2026-08-20/), [Al Jazeera, Aug 21](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/8/21/brazil-launches-ai-supercomputer-push-while-balancing-us-and-chinese-tech)). It is the most concrete step yet in Brazil's national AI plan, and a striking example of Global South procurement diplomacy.

### Russia: GigaChat Gets a Voice

Sber updated GigaChat on August 5 with voice dialog, memory and a research mode, moving the assistant into a new class of interaction ([AXIOMA AI](https://axioma-ai.ru/ai-russia/gigachat-voice-dialog-august-5-2026-20260814)), with official API access for developers ([AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.ru/obzory-prilozhenij/gigachat-ot-sbera-v-2026-godu-kak-polzovatsya-api-tarify-i-sravnenie-s-yandexgpt.html)). The month also brought a public spat: Sber accused Yandex's YandexGPT of not being "truly Russian," claiming GigaChat is built on proprietary architecture and data without borrowed weights; Yandex denies this, saying its models are built from scratch ([AXIOMA AI](https://axioma-ai.ru/ai-russia/sber-vs-yandex-real-russian-ai-dispute-2026-20260816)). With Western chips and models sanctioned, both camps are marketing domestic provenance hard — a trend worth watching.

### The West: A Relatively Quiet Month

Out west, the action was more corporate than technical: OpenAI and Anthropic are openly competing on enterprise privacy guarantees, with OpenAI rolling out new customer data protections ([TechCrunch, Aug 19](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/19/openai-seeks-to-one-up-anthropic-with-new-customer-privacy-protections/)), while fresh data suggests Anthropic still leads OpenAI among business users — a lead it took in May and hasn't given back ([TechCrunch, Aug 20](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/20/openai-is-gaining-on-anthropic-with-business-users-new-data-indicates/)).

## What It Means

The throughline this month is diversification: Chinese open weights, European regulation, Gulf compute, African policy and Latin American procurement are all pulling the AI map into a genuinely multipolar shape. For builders in Kenya and across Africa, the practical takeaway is that frontier capability is getting cheaper and more accessible by the week — and that local policy, capital and talent pipelines are finally getting the attention they deserve.
