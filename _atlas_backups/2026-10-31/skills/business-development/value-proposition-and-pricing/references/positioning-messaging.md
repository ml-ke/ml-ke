# Positioning and Messaging

You cannot price a value the buyer does not perceive, so sharpening positioning comes
before pricing.

## The positioning statement

Use this exact form, one per priority persona:

```
For [priority persona] who [situation or job to be done],
we are the [category] that [key capability],
so they can [outcome],
unlike [alternative].
```

The two words that do the most work are **category** (the shelf the buyer searches on) and
**alternative** (what they would do otherwise — often "nothing"). Get those right before you
polish anything else. A weak category forces the buyer to invent one; a weak alternative
makes the value feel optional.

## Message hierarchy

Turn the statement into one usable message block:

1. **Headline claim** — one sentence, the primary outcome.
2. **Three proof points** — each carries a metric or a named mechanism, never an adjective.
   - Weak: "fast and reliable". Strong: "statutory filing closes 6 days earlier, on the 9th".
3. **One call to action** — a single next step, matched to the persona and the stage.

## Persona variants

Write one version per buying-committee role (bd-context section 4), because each is measured
on a different outcome:

| Persona | Cares about | Lead with |
|---------|-------------|-----------|
| Economic buyer | cost, risk, strategy | the quantified business case |
| Champion | making themselves look good | the outcome that advances *their* goal |
| User | ease, doing their job | time saved and pain removed |
| Technical influencer | fit, security, integration | mechanism and proof |
| Blocker | change risk | migration path and guarantees |

## The "so what / says who" test

Pass every claim through two questions:

- **So what?** Does it state an outcome the buyer values, in their terms?
- **Says who?** Is there a metric, a reference, or a mechanism behind it?

A claim that fails either question is cut or rewritten. A block of claims that all pass is
the whole message.

## Message testing

Before it ships, test the message cheaply:

1. **Read it to a real customer** and ask them to repeat it back. If they cannot, it is too
   abstract.
2. **Watch it in discovery** — does the prospect's own language start mirroring yours? That
   is the signal it landed.
3. **A/B it in outbound** and measure reply and meeting rates, not open rates.
4. Record the version and date of any message that worked; messages rot as the market moves.

## Traps

1. **Positioning on features.** Buyers buy outcomes; features are proof, not the claim.
2. **A category no one searches on.** If you invent a category, you own the education cost.
3. **Ignoring the status quo as the alternative.** For most deals the real competitor is
   inertia; position against the cost of doing nothing.
4. **One message for the whole committee.** The CFO and the end user do not buy the same
   thing; give each their own version.
5. **Adjectives instead of numbers.** "Best-in-class" is noise; a metric is a claim.
