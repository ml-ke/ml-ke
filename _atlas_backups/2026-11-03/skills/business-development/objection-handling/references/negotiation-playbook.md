# Negotiation playbook — detail

Reference for `objection-handling`. Load when planning a negotiation or coaching a
rep through a stalled deal. Core steps live in `SKILL.md`.

## 1. BATNA — your best alternative to a negotiated agreement

BATNA is what you do if this deal does not happen. It is the source of your leverage
and the reason you can hold a floor without bluffing.

- **Weak BATNA (danger):** only one deal in the pipeline, or a quarter-end number that
  depends on it. This is when reps over-discount. Counter it by widening the pipeline
  before negotiating, not by lowering the price during negotiation.
- **Strong BATNA:** several qualified deals, a product-led path, or a serviceable
  smaller scope. You can walk without losing the number.
- **Their BATNA** sets your ceiling. If their best alternative is "do nothing" and
  the pain is real, your ceiling is high. If their best alternative is a cheaper
  competitor that already meets their criteria, your ceiling is their price — so
  change the criteria (see competition objections in `SKILL.md`).

Write your BATNA down as one sentence: "If we do not sign X by Y, we will ___."

## 2. ZOPA — the zone of possible agreement

The ZOPA is the overlap between your walk-away and their maximum willingness.

```
their max  <----->  your walk-away
   |                      |
   |==== ZOPA if their max > your walk-away ====|
```

- If their max is below your walk-away, there is **no ZOPA at this price**. Do not
  split the difference into a loss. Instead change the variables: reduce scope,
  extend term, adjust timing, or walk.
- Estimate their max from evidence — budget stated in discovery, what they pay an
  incumbent, their cost of the problem. Mark it as an assumption; it is rarely known
  exactly.

## 3. Concession strategy

- **Never concede first** unless you are anchoring deliberately with a high opening.
- **Decreasing increments:** a large first concession followed by smaller ones signals
  you are approaching your floor. Equal concessions invite an endless "split the
  difference".
- **Every concession is conditional:** "I can do X **if** you can do Y." Unconditional
  giving teaches the buyer that persistence beats your stated floor.
- **Label the concession as final in scope, not in tone:** "this is the furthest I can
  go on price" is credible only if you have held the previous rungs.
- **Trade non-price first.** Terms, timeline, references, and prepayment are cheaper
  than price and often worth more to the buyer.

### Concession value cheat-sheet

| Concession | Cost to you | Often worth to buyer | Prefer when |
|------------|-------------|----------------------|-------------|
| Faster onboarding slot | Low (scheduling) | High (time-to-value) | Buyer is deadline-driven |
| Extended validity window | Very low | Medium (breathing room) | Buyer's process is slow |
| Named reference request | Low (may cost goodwill) | Medium | You need proof assets anyway |
| Annual prepay discount | Cash-flow positive | High | Buyer has budget now |
| Multi-year price lock | Medium (upside risk) | High (budget certainty) | You want a longer commitment |
| Per-seat discount | Direct margin | Medium | Buyer commits to more seats |
| Below-floor price | Margin and precedent | High | Only with leadership approval + a major trade |

## 4. Worked negotiation example

Deal: a 128-seat clinical roster, list KES 89,600/month, floor set at 12% below list.
Buyer: "Your price is 20% above the other vendor; we need a discount."

1. **Classify.** Price objection, but diagnose first: "Is it the total, or how you
   would fund it?" Buyer reveals the comparison is on a stripped-down feature set.
2. **Reframe.** "The other quote does not include the fatigue guardrails or statutory
   filing. What would a single compliance miss cost you?" Buyer concedes it is the
   headline number, not the feature set.
3. **Trade, do not cut.** Offer rung 2 then 3: extended validity plus a reference in
   exchange for annual prepayment — which lands a larger TCV than the 20% discount
   they asked for, at a smaller effective concession.
4. **Hold the floor.** If they push below floor, offer rung 1 (faster onboarding) and
   a scope adjustment instead of price.
5. **Close.** Book the joint call with the economic buyer; the champion cannot carry a
   price exception alone.

## 5. Closing tactics

| Situation | Tactic | Why |
|-----------|--------|-----|
| Signal of readiness | Direct ask: "Shall we schedule kickoff?" | Removes ambiguity; forces the decision |
| Silent after proposal | Dated nudge tied to validity | Creates a natural, non-pushy deadline |
| Multiple stakeholders | Joint call with the economic buyer | Stop the game of telephone |
| Repeated deferral | Dated mutual action plan | An undated "later" is a no |
| Price standstill | Trade a rung, not a cut | Preserves price integrity |
| Walk-away decision | Say it once, calmly, and mean it | A bluff you cannot hold destroys credibility |

## 6. Negotiation post-mortem

After the deal closes (won or lost), capture: what the real objection turned out to
be, which concession was most effective, whether the floor held, and the actual ZOPA.
Feed recurring objections back into the library and, if they are positioning-driven,
to `value-proposition-and-pricing`. Post-decision pattern analysis belongs to
`win-loss-review`.
