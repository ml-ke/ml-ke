# Teardown Method

A fixed structure keeps battlecards comparable across competitors. Run this once per
competitor in scope; do not let the format drift.

## 1. Source hierarchy

Gather evidence in this priority order, and record source + date for every claim:

1. **Their public pricing and feature pages.** The primary fact base. Quote and cite.
2. **Their docs and changelog.** Reveals real capability and shipping cadence.
3. **Their job postings.** Roadmap signal — what they are building next.
4. **Customer reviews** (G2, Capterra, TrustRadius). The buyer's words, including complaints.
5. **Your own CRM win-loss notes and call recordings.** Where the buyer names them directly.
6. **Funding, press, and leadership posts.** Direction and runway.

Never restate their marketing as fact. Write "they claim X" and cite the URL.

## 2. Teardown checklist

For each competitor, capture all of the following. Leave a field blank rather than guess.

- [ ] **Positioning** — their one-line claim, quoted and cited.
- [ ] **ICP** — who they actually sell to (from their site, case studies, or pricing tiers).
- [ ] **Packaging and pricing model** — metric, tier count, price points (public only).
- [ ] **Product strengths** — the two or three things they genuinely do well.
- [ ] **Product weaknesses** — gaps a buyer would care about, with evidence.
- [ ] **Our counter** — where we win, mapped to the buyer's decision criteria.
- [ ] **Proof** — a metric, reference, or guarantee that backs the counter.
- [ ] **Landmines** — claims that backfire, or their known traps.

## 3. Confidence scoring

Score each non-obvious claim high, medium, or low.

- **High** — stated on their public site, or confirmed by a buyer in a recorded deal.
- **Medium** — from a review, a partner, or consistent secondhand signal.
- **Low** — inferred from a job posting, a leak, or pattern-matching. Mark `[INFERRED]`.

Do not put a low-confidence claim on the battlecard without the `[INFERRED]` tag. A rep
who repeats an inference as fact gets caught in front of the buyer.

## 4. Win/loss theme extraction

Do this from your own deals, not external summaries:

1. Filter closed-won and closed-lost deals where the competitor appeared (last 2-4 quarters).
2. Tag the deciding reason per deal: price, feature, trust, incumbent relationship,
   procurement, timing.
3. Count the tags. The top two become "win them on / lose them on" on the card.
4. Attach one verbatim buyer quote per theme.
5. If you have fewer than about five deals, mark `[THIN EVIDENCE]` and stop. Do not
   manufacture a pattern from two data points.

## 5. Refresh cadence

Competitive facts rot fast. Re-verify pricing and feature claims quarterly, and immediately
after any competitor launch, funding round, or price change. Stamp **Last verified** on every
card; a card with no date should not be trusted in a live deal.

## 6. What stays off the card

- Speculation about their roadmap with no evidence.
- Their private pricing scraped from a leak (a legal and trust risk).
- Feature-by-feature comparison walls that no rep will read mid-call.
- Anything you would not want the buyer to see you assert.
