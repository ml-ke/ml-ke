# MEDDPICC

Detail for `discovery-call` section 4. Eight fields; a deal with a blank field
is a deal with an unpriced risk. Fill every letter before you forecast or
propose. Source: standard enterprise sales qualification framework, as taught
by Force Management.

## M - Metrics

- **What:** the quantified business outcome the buyer wants, and who owns that number.
- **Ask:** "What does <problem> cost you today — in hours, money, or risk?"
- **Capture:** the number, the unit, and whose target it rolls into.
- **Disqualify if:** the buyer cannot name any metric and has no owner for one. You
  are selling to a preference, not a business case.

## E - Economic buyer

- **What:** the person who can approve the budget and stop the deal.
- **Ask:** "Who else needs to be comfortable with this, and who signs?"
- **Capture:** name and role; whether you have met them.
- **Disqualify if:** nobody will name the approver after two attempts — the deal
  has no path to a signature.

## D - Decision criteria

- **What:** how the buyer will judge the options (and any formal scorecard).
- **Ask:** "What will you compare us against, and what matters most?"
- **Capture:** the criteria in priority order.
- **Disqualify if:** the criteria are unstated and the buyer refuses to engage —
  you are bidding blind.

## D - Decision process

- **What:** the steps and people between today and a decision.
- **Ask:** "Walk me through what happens between now and a signed contract."
- **Capture:** the sequence, each gate, and the expected dates.
- **Disqualify if:** the process is open-ended with no next gate.

## P - Paper process

- **What:** procurement, legal, security review, and the signature path.
- **Ask:** "Who handles procurement and legal, and how long do they take?"
- **Capture:** each reviewer, the order, and typical duration.
- **Disqualify if:** you discover a 12-week legal review the week you expect to close.

## I - Identify pain

- **What:** the problem and its quantified cost, in the buyer's own words.
- **Ask:** "Where does <process> break, and what does that cost you?"
- **Capture:** verbatim. Paraphrase loses the language the proposal must echo.
- **Disqualify if:** the pain is trivial or nobody is accountable for fixing it.

## C - Champion

- **What:** someone with power and a personal stake who sells for you internally.
- **Ask:** "How would you describe the value to your boss?"
- **Capture:** who they are and what they have actually done (arranged a meeting,
  shared a scorecard). A friendly contact is not a champion.
- **Disqualify if:** nobody will act. No champion, no deal.

## C - Competition

- **What:** the named alternatives plus the status quo ("do nothing").
- **Ask:** "What else are you looking at, and what happens if you change nothing?"
- **Capture:** every option, including the incumbent and the spreadsheet.
- **Disqualify if:** you are the only option and the status quo is free — often a sign
  the buyer is not serious.

## Using the map

- After the call, list the blank letters in the CRM; they are the plan for the next touch.
- Forecast confidence tracks coverage: a deal with E, P, and C blank is not a
  commit, regardless of enthusiasm.
- When two letters conflict (criteria say X, paper process needs Y), resolve before
  proposing.
