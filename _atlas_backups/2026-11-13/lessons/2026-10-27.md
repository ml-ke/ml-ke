## 1. What was learned

### 1.1 arxiv 2602.12430 — *Agent Skills for LLMs: Architecture, Acquisition, Security* (survey) — HIGH
Four-axis survey (architecture / acquisition / deployment / security). The actionable part is its **Skill Trust and Lifecycle Governance Framework: a four-tier, gate-based permission model that maps provenance to graduated deployment capabilities**, plus seven named open challenges (cross-platform portability, capability-based permissions first among them). Independently restates the 26.1%-of-community-skills-vulnerable figure we already carry.
- Diff vs Hermes: we have provenance *hashing* (AST07 drift) but no notion of a skill's capability tier, so an unrecorded skill that reads `~/.hermes/.env` and posts somewhere ranks the same as a prose-only checklist.
- Adoptable: tier the library by capability, and let the tier decide what needs recording. **Adopted** (see 2.1).

### 1.2 arxiv 2607.10113 — *Dynamic Agent Skills: A Lifecycle Survey* (124 papers, 2023–2026) — HIGH
Skill libraries are *lifecycle-managed, verified, evolving artifact stores*, with an **8-stage lifecycle**: evidence acquisition → proposal → verification/admission → storage → retrieval/composition → maintenance (repair/prune) → distillation/portability → governance.
- Reported findings: **admission and repair are repeatedly the decisive stages**; **flat retrieval degrades as the library grows**; and benchmarks **under-report library trajectories, usage–utility gaps, and safety surfaces**.
- Diff vs Hermes: our pipeline covers admission (scan+triage), retrieval (57-char routing), evolution (bridge stamp/seam/provenance). **Repair** is delegated to the curator, and we had never audited what a prune *breaks*. That gap produced the week's real defect (1.4).

### 1.3 morluto/rea — 418,107★, TypeScript, community repo, pushed daily — MEDIUM-HIGH (stars = discovery, not trust)
Reverse-engineering agent skill suite (`.agents/skills/reverse-engineer-anything`). Best-written capability boundary encountered so far:
